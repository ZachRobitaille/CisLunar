See how CisLunar would compare two landing scenarios, from choosing a site to reading sunlight and Earth visibility windows.

How it works

Example: the landing date slips. Keep Shackleton’s rim as the reference site and compare the original date with the new one.

  1. Choose a site

    Select a reference site or enter lunar coordinates. This example uses Shackleton’s rim.

  2. Set up two scenarios

    Enter a start date and time in UTC and choose a duration. To isolate the effect of the delay, keep the duration fixed and change only the start date.

  3. Simulate the Sun and Earth

    Calculate their directions and elevations at each time. Compare each one with the horizon in its direction to identify where terrain blocks the view.

  4. Compare the windows

    Use the same time scale to compare sunlight duration, Earth visibility windows and the time remaining before either body drops below the horizon.

Planned data and validation

Proposed sources include JPL Horizons for Sun and Earth positions and LOLA elevation maps for south pole terrain. The horizon calculation would account for terrain and lunar curvature in the absence of atmospheric refraction.

Angles would be checked against reference ephemerides across several sites and dates. Terrain resolution and time sampling limit the precision of the estimated windows.

The solar indicator will depend on panel orientation and the model used. Earth visibility indicates a geometrically possible direct link. Assessing the radio link also requires antenna, power and network information.
