The data and tools already exist. CisLunar targets a faster way to compare sites and dates through a shared view of the Sun, Earth and terrain.

ALREADY USED IN MISSIONS

Two ways to reach Earth.

DIRECT LINK · GROUND NETWORKS

Antennas on Earth

From the near side, a vehicle can communicate directly with a ground station if the path is clear. NASA's Deep Space Network supports lunar missions among others; communications from the lunar surface date back to Apollo.

What it enables: commands, telemetry and science data without an intermediate lunar relay.

Limitations to consider

Limitations: the Moon and its terrain can block the path. Pointing, power and available antenna time must be planned. A large antenna on Earth cannot see through the Moon.

NASA · Deep Space Network ↗

ORBITAL RELAY · DEMONSTRATED EXAMPLE

Queqiao and Chang’e 4

In 2019, Chang’e 4 landed in Von Kármán crater on the far side. The Queqiao satellite provided the communications relay to Earth, demonstrating a route around the Moon's obstruction.

What it enables: a radio path to a region with no direct view of Earth.

Limitations to consider

Limitations: a relay is designed for specific geometries and missions. This example does not mean every rover can connect to it or that all craters are covered. Compatibility, local visibility and capacity still need to be checked.

IN DEVELOPMENT

Networks in development

Moonlight and NASA's lunar relays

ESA is developing Moonlight to provide lunar communications and navigation services. NASA's LCRNS programme also aims to extend communications to hard-to-reach regions, including the south pole and the far side.

These programmes are in development. They do not guarantee continuous coverage or access to a service for our project.

Their effectiveness will depend on orbits, local obstacles, compatible terminals and service access. A constellation does not remove the need to study each site.
